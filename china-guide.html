// Plan Your Trip

Practical essentials.

The things that quietly make or break a China trip. Current for 2026 — but rules move, so we confirm yours before you book.

01

Visa & Entry

Depends on your passport. Most EU & UK travelers: 30-day visa-free. US, Canada & Australia: 240-hour (10-day) visa-free transit, or a multi-entry visa for standard trips. Israeli passports: a China visa, with long-validity multi-entry facilitation available.

VARIES BY PASSPORT
02

Best Time to Visit

Spring (Mar–May) and autumn (Sep–Nov) are ideal — mild weather, clear skies. Avoid the Golden Week holidays around the first days of May and October, when everything is packed.

SPRING · AUTUMN
03

Getting Around

The world's largest high-speed-rail network (40,000+ km, up to 350 km/h) links every hub, alongside world-class airports and city metros. City-to-city in hours, not a day.

HIGH-SPEED RAIL · AIR
04

Money & Payments

China is effectively cashless. Foreign Visa/Mastercard now link to both Alipay and WeChat Pay (quick passport verification). Set up both apps before you fly, and keep some RMB cash for small vendors.

ALIPAY · WECHAT PAY
05

Connectivity

Some Western apps and sites are restricted. Bring an international eSIM/roaming plan or a reputable VPN, and install WeChat before you arrive — on the ground, it's essential for messaging, payments and more.

ESIM · VPN · WECHAT
06

Language & Safety

Mandarin is the language; English signage is common at airports, stations and hotels, and translation apps fill the gaps. China is among the safest destinations for travelers. Emergency: 110 police · 120 medical · 119 fire.

SAFE · ENGLISH SIGNAGE
