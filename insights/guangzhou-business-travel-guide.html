Destinations · Guangzhou 广州

Guangzhou for business travelers: the gateway to south China.

Two thousand years of trade, the country's biggest fair, and the densest manufacturing region on earth an hour in every direction. A working briefing for buyers, importers and anyone whose supply chain runs through the Pearl River Delta.

By Sino Business Partner Updated August 2026 8 min read Destinations
Key facts, in short
  • Guangzhou is the capital of Guangdong province and the trade capital of southern China — historically the terminus of the Maritime Silk Road.
  • Home of the Canton Fair, China's largest import and export fair, held each spring and autumn at the Pazhou complex.
  • Main airport: Guangzhou Baiyun International (CAN). Main rail hub: Guangzhou South, with high-speed connections to Shenzhen, Hong Kong and the national network.
  • Best business base: Zhujiang New Town (Tianhe) for balance; Pazhou if your business is fair-centred.
  • The Pearl River Delta — Dongguan, Foshan, Shenzhen — is within one to two hours, making Guangzhou a natural base for factory visits.
  • Cantonese cuisine originates here. A dim sum lunch is one of the most effective client meetings available to you.

Why Guangzhou matters

Guangzhou has been trading with the outside world for over two thousand years. It was the southern terminus of the Maritime Silk Road, the single port through which foreign trade was funnelled for long stretches of the Qing dynasty, and it remains the commercial centre of Guangdong — the province that turned China into the world's manufacturing floor.

For a Western business traveler, Guangzhou is rarely a destination in itself. It is a hub: the place you base yourself while working suppliers, attending the Canton Fair, and reaching factories across the Pearl River Delta. That distinction should shape how you plan the trip.

The city is also notably comfortable to work in. It is subtropical, relaxed by Chinese metropolitan standards, exceptionally good at food, and has a long institutional habit of dealing with foreigners — which shows in the hotels, the interpreters and the general ease of doing business.

Getting there and getting around

Arriving

  • Guangzhou Baiyun International Airport (CAN) is the main gateway, with wide international connectivity and extensive domestic service. It is connected to the city by metro and expressway.
  • By high-speed rail — Guangzhou South station links to Shenzhen in around half an hour, to Hong Kong via the cross-boundary service, and to the national network north.
  • Via Hong Kong is a common approach for long-haul travelers, particularly where direct flights to CAN are limited. The onward rail or coach connection is straightforward, but budget time for the border crossing.

Moving around

The Guangzhou Metro is extensive, clean, signposted in English and by far the most reliable way to cross the city at peak hours. Metro Line 8 serves Pazhou, which is the line that matters if you are attending the Canton Fair.

For taxis, use DiDi — it has an English interface and removes the language friction entirely. Keep destination addresses saved in Chinese characters to show drivers directly.

Timing note

Guangzhou is subtropical. Summer (roughly June to September) is hot and humid with a real typhoon season; spring and autumn are markedly more comfortable. This is one reason the Canton Fair sits in April/May and October/November.

The general rule for Chinese cities applies with force here: book by district, not by price. Guangzhou is large, and a poorly located hotel will cost you an hour and a half every day.

Working the Pearl River Delta

This is the real reason to be in Guangzhou. Within one to two hours by road or rail you can reach an extraordinary concentration of manufacturing:

  • Dongguan — electronics, furniture, footwear, plastics. Long-standing export manufacturing base.
  • Foshan — ceramics, sanitary ware, furniture, home fittings, aluminium.
  • Shenzhen — electronics, hardware, prototyping. Around 30 minutes by high-speed rail.
  • Zhongshan and Jiangmen — lighting, hardware, appliances.

Practical guidance for factory days:

  • Group visits geographically. Two factories in Foshan on one day beats one in Foshan and one in Dongguan.
  • Confirm in writing 48 hours ahead, and again that morning. Schedules move, and a confirmed visit that nobody re-checked is the most common wasted day of a sourcing trip.
  • Bring a technical interpreter. Factory conversations turn on specifications, tolerances and certifications — precisely where general English fails.
  • Ask to see the production line, not just the showroom. A trading company with a rented showroom and a manufacturer with a real line look identical in a catalogue.
  • Photograph and document. Certifications, line conditions, sample labelling — you will want this later.

Dining and client entertaining

Guangzhou is the home of Cantonese cuisine, and food carries genuine business weight here. Underrating this is a mistake — a well-chosen restaurant communicates seriousness in a way a well-chosen slide deck does not.

Dim sum lunch (饮茶)

The most useful business meal available to you in this city. It is informal enough to build rapport, structured enough to hold a real conversation, and unmistakably local — which reads as respect. Traditionally a morning-to-midday affair; the classic tea houses in the older districts are worth the detour over a hotel restaurant.

The evening banquet

If your counterpart hosts, accept. Expect a round table, shared dishes, and toasts. Let the host order and direct seating. Reciprocating on a later trip is well received and remembered.

Practical notes

  • Cantonese cooking prizes freshness and subtlety over heat — it is one of the easiest Chinese cuisines for Western palates.
  • Flag dietary restrictions in advance, through your interpreter or host, rather than at the table.
  • Tipping is not customary.
  • Book ahead for anything of quality during Canton Fair weeks — the whole city fills.

The free half-day

Guangzhou rewards a few hours off more than most business destinations, because everything worth seeing is close together:

  • Canton Tower — the city's landmark; the observation levels give you the whole Pearl River Delta in one view.
  • Shamian Island — the former foreign concession, all colonial architecture and shaded avenues. Fifteen quiet minutes that recalibrate a hard trip.
  • Chen Clan Ancestral Hall — the finest surviving example of Lingnan architecture and craftsmanship in the city.
  • A Pearl River night cruise — the standard client evening, and it works. The skyline is genuinely spectacular after dark.
  • Yuexiu Park and the old city markets — the version of Guangzhou that existed long before the CBD.

Practical notes

  • Language: Cantonese is the local language; Mandarin is universally understood. English is common in international hotels and thin elsewhere — translation apps and an interpreter cover the gap.
  • Payments: Alipay and WeChat Pay are essential. Set both up before arrival — see the preparation checklist.
  • Connectivity: arrange international roaming or an eSIM; assume Western apps will not work on local networks.
  • Safety: Guangzhou is a very safe city, including at night. Normal urban caution in crowded markets is sufficient.
  • Emergency: 110 police · 120 medical · 119 fire.
  • Timing: avoid Golden Week (early May and early October) — domestic travel peaks and many factories close.

Guangzhou works best when it is treated as an operating base rather than a stop — hotel in the right district, factory visits grouped and confirmed, an interpreter who knows your category, and a dinner that does the relationship work. Frequently asked questions

Why do business travelers go to Guangzhou?

Guangzhou is the trade capital of southern China and the host city of the Canton Fair, China's largest import and export fair. It also serves as the natural base for visiting factories across the Pearl River Delta — Dongguan, Foshan, Shenzhen and Zhongshan are all within one to two hours — making it the key hub for importers, buyers and sourcing teams.

Where is the best area to stay in Guangzhou for business?

Zhujiang New Town in Tianhe district is the best all-round choice: it is the modern CBD with international hotels, strong dining and good metro access, roughly 20 to 30 minutes from the Canton Fair complex. Pazhou is the better choice if your trip is built entirely around the fair, though rates spike and rooms sell out early during sessions.

How do I get from Guangzhou to Shenzhen or Hong Kong?

High-speed rail from Guangzhou South station reaches Shenzhen in around 30 minutes and connects to Hong Kong via the cross-boundary service. This makes it practical to base yourself in Guangzhou and take day trips into the wider Pearl River Delta.

What is the best way to get around Guangzhou?

The Guangzhou Metro is extensive, English-signposted and the most reliable option at peak hours; Line 8 serves Pazhou and the Canton Fair complex. For taxis, the DiDi app has an English interface. Keep destination addresses in Chinese characters to show drivers.

What should I know about business dinners in Guangzhou?

Guangzhou is the home of Cantonese cuisine and food carries real business weight. A dim sum lunch is one of the most effective business meetings available in the city — informal enough to build rapport, local enough to signal respect. For evening banquets, let your host order and direct seating, expect toasts, and note that tipping is not customary in mainland China.

When is the best time to visit Guangzhou?

Spring and autumn are the most comfortable seasons. Guangzhou is subtropical, with hot, humid summers and a typhoon season roughly from June to September. The Canton Fair sessions in April–May and October–November fall in the pleasant windows, though hotel rates rise sharply during them.
